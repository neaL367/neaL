Hi, I'm Atichat. Friends call me Art.

I'm in my final year of information technology at Sripatum University in Bangkok, graduating this December. I write software, I make pixel art, and outside of that I'm usually playing something.

## The reason I started

I've loved games for as long as I can remember. Not casually, either. Some of my clearest memories as a kid are wrapped up in them.

What bothers me now is how much of what I play doesn't feel like that anymore. Subscriptions, pay-to-win systems, optimisation disguised as design, quality decided by a spreadsheet. **Somewhere along the way a lot of games stopped being made for the person playing them.**

I don't think that's a small complaint. The games I loved gave me something, and I want to make the game I actually want to play, built the way I think it should be built.

## The loop I keep falling into

I've tried before. More than once.

I'd start something, get a few months in, cancel it. Start again, remake what I'd already made, give up. That cycle has eaten whole years and I'm not going to pretend it didn't.

> Every one of those attempts taught me something. None of them were wasted, and none of them were finished.

This time is different in one way that matters: I'm not treating it as a sprint I can lose. It's a long road, and I'm staying on it.

## What I'm building

An isometric open-world game with a medieval setting. It has no name yet, which is fine. I'd rather the thing exist before it has a title.

It's the project I've been circling for years, and I'm building it in the open time around everything else.

## Right now

I'm working and studying at the same time, which leaves less room than I'd like. After December that changes: I start full time as a developer next year, and the game becomes what I do with my evenings and weekends.

That's the honest version. Not a launch date, just a plan.

I'm looking forward to it. Let's build this thing.
