Over the past few months, I spent significant time experimenting with what many developers now call "vibe coding"—relying on large language models and prompt-driven workflows to scaffold features, resolve bugs, and iterate quickly.

It is undeniable how empowering modern AI tools feel at first glance. You type a prompt describing what you want, and seconds later, a complete component or function appears on your screen. But as I leaned further into this workflow, I realized that relying solely on intuition and generative AI comes with distinct tradeoffs that every developer needs to recognize.

## The Illusion of Instant Velocity

When you prompt your way through an entire project without inspecting the generated code, your initial velocity skyrockets. You bypass reading documentation, you skip debugging with breakpoints, and you avoid wrestling with tricky edge cases.

However, speed without comprehension is fragile. When you delegate all problem-solving to an AI model, you gradually lose touch with how your application actually functions. When a subtle state bug or memory leak inevitably happens in production, you find yourself unable to debug code you never truly understood in the first place.

> If you do not read and comprehend the code you commit, you are not developing software—you are simply outsourcing your judgment.

True engineering is not just about outputting lines of code; it is about building mental models of how systems interact, fail, and scale.

## Reframing AI as an Accelerated Learning Partner

The real breakthrough came when I shifted how I interact with AI. Instead of using it as a ghostwriter to blindly generate code, I began treating it as an interactive pair-programmer and research assistant.

When encountering unfamiliar libraries or complex algorithms, ask questions that challenge both the model and your understanding:

- *Why is this specific pattern or data structure recommended here?*
- *What are the performance implications and potential memory bottlenecks?*
- *Can you explain how this function behaves when network conditions fail?*
- *What are alternative architectural approaches and their tradeoffs?*

When used this way, AI transforms into a tireless tutor. It compresses the time required to understand complex concepts and encourages you to read official documentation with deeper curiosity.

## Principles I Follow When Coding with AI

1. **Read before committing:** Always trace every execution path, check variable scopes, and verify error boundaries before merging code into your branch.
2. **Own the architecture:** Use AI for boilerplate and exploratory snippets, but keep ownership of data flow, domain models, and high-level system design.
3. **Document what you learn:** Take active notes on new patterns, APIs, and edge cases you discover during each session. Turning fleeting insights into written knowledge cements your skills.

Tools will continue to evolve, but the fundamentals of problem solving, disciplined curiosity, and clear thinking remain irreplaceable.
