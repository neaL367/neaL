From January 5 to April 24, 2026, I worked as a software developer at TQM through my university's co-operative education program. It was my first time inside an enterprise engineering team, working on systems that real people use.

Four months later, I think differently about code, teamwork, and growth.

## Starting Out

Day one was intimidating. Building hobby projects alone is nothing like joining a live codebase with real users, business rules, and infrastructure you did not design.

At first I stayed quiet and careful. Surrounded by systems I did not understand, I learned out of necessity. I asked questions, read old pull requests, and slowly found my footing.

## How Software Really Gets Built

Before TQM, my workflow was simple: write code, run it locally, push. At TQM I saw the whole cycle:

1. **Investigation** — Understand the business problem and the user's pain before writing code.
2. **Requirements and design** — Clarify specs, define data contracts, plan the architecture.
3. **Implementation** — Write clean code that follows team standards.
4. **Testing** — Cover edge cases with unit tests and catch regressions early.
5. **User acceptance testing** — Validate the work with stakeholders in staging.
6. **Deployment and monitoring** — Ship safely, then watch the health metrics.

Writing code turned out to be about a third of the job. Communication, testing, and operational discipline matter just as much.

## A Steady Rhythm

Every day started with a 15-minute standup:

- What I finished yesterday
- What I will do today
- What is blocking me or needs another team

Short and predictable, but it kept everyone aligned and made asking for help normal.

## Autonomy with a Safety Net

What I liked most was the balance between freedom and support. Developers picked their own tools and patterns, and the focus stayed on outcomes instead of process for its own sake.

When I got stuck, senior engineers jumped in. They did not just hand me the answer — they explained why a design choice made sense for the wider system. Those reviews were the best lessons of the internship.

## The People

Debugging a regression at release time, then celebrating a clean deploy with the other interns — that is what I remember. Stress, jokes, and small wins, shared.

Four months went fast. To any student considering a co-op: take the leap. Shipping real software next to experienced engineers teaches things a classroom cannot.

Thank you to the team at TQM.
